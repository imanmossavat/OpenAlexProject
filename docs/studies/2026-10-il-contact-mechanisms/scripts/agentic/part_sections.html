Who works on contact-rich imitation learning

Two populations, both from OpenAlex. The seed citers are the 1,450 works that cite at least one of crawl A's seeds. Most cite Diffusion Policy (1,127) or ACT (663), so they describe the wider imitation-learning community. The field set is narrower: the 217 works from 2023 onward whose titles pair an imitation-learning term with a contact term (force, tactile, insertion, assembly, compliance and similar). The exact query is in scripts/agentic/fetch_all.py.

Authors who cite at least three of the four seeds that have citers (FACTR, robomimic, Diffusion Policy, ACT) include Cewu Lu (18 citing papers; FoAR and RDP come from his group), Hiroshi Ito and Tetsuya Ogata (12 and 10, the only ones in this list who cite FACTR), Yuke Zhu (11), Sergey Levine (10) and Chelsea Finn (10).

Coverage by year: the blind spot on a larger set

The 36 evidence papers are a small sample, so the same test was repeated on the 217-work field set, which was defined independently of the crawls. It shows the same pattern. 95 of 217 works (44%) have neither references nor citations in OpenAlex, and in 2026 the share is 66 of 103 (64%). Of the 122 works with links, crawl A reached 83 (68%) and crawl B 87 (71%).

Topics in the literature

The corpus is 1,622 works: the seed citers, the field set and the evidence papers, de-duplicated by title. 1,197 have an abstract in OpenAlex. Titles and abstracts were embedded with SPECTER, reduced with UMAP to 8 dimensions and clustered with k-means. The silhouette score was best at k=6 (0.389). k=11 (0.363) was used because it separates tactile policies, assembly, surgical robotics and teleoperation hardware, which k=6 merges. Keywords come from class-based TF-IDF. The topic names are Claude's, from the keywords and the papers nearest each cluster centre. The method is described in docs/studies/agentic-analyses.md.

How to read it. The field set (contact-rich titles) sits mostly in three topics: assembly and insertion (64), tactile and force-aware policies (46), and contact-rich bimanual compliant policies (36). The mechanism evidence sits elsewhere. Most of it (robomimic, Diffusion Policy, Much Ado About Noising, the Pitfalls paper, MILE, IntervenGen, MinInter) falls in "IL theory, data and interventions", which contains only 4 field-set works. In other words, the papers that establish mechanisms are general imitation-learning papers, not contact-rich ones. A search restricted to contact-rich titles would miss them.

Agentic analysis

Crawl B reached 7 field-set works that crawl A did not, and crawl A none that B missed. Two of the seven were search seeds (QDP, ContactDP); the others came through links, for example VIDP, rebar-cage assembly and DMP-based contact-rich teleoperation. Most of crawl B's extra papers entered as references of older learning-from-demonstration surveys: Learning and generalization of motor skills by learning from demonstration (2009) brought in 484, Robot Learning from Demonstration (2012) 461, and Robot Learning from Demonstration in Robotic Assembly: A Survey (2018) 227. That is volume, not relevance, and it matches the NCA finding that more papers does not mean more of the topic.
