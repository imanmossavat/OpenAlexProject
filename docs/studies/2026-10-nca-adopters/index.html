Does a citation crawler beat agentic search? A test on neural cellular automata

Prepared for the Fontys ICT Interaction Design lectorate and for readers of this repository. The test question is which fields, labs and companies use neural cellular automata (NCA). The real question is how well ArticleCrawler gathers a field's literature compared with agentic search: ChatGPT deep research, and Claude's own short web search. Put simply: if you add all this machinery, is it any better than asking Claude to search?

Authorship and provenance. This study was carried out and written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI agent from Anthropic, working in a Claude Code session. Claude is not part of the ArticleCrawler repository and is not one of its authors. It used the repository as a tool, the way any user would. Each figure carries a label saying who produced it.

Bottom line

  • Yes, the crawler finds more, but not the newest work. The best results came from using search and the crawler together. The test used a checklist of 14 NCA application domains. Claude's 3-minute web search and ChatGPT deep research each covered 5 domains fully and 2 partly, plus games, which was already in the brief. ChatGPT also made at least 4 factual errors. Each crawl's data covered 10 or 11 domains fully and 2 or 3 partly. All three crawls missed only one domain: language models.
  • Seeding the crawler from search worked best. One crawl was seeded with the 14 papers Claude's search had named, on top of the 4 brief seeds. That crawl reached 96 of the 210 OpenAlex works with "neural cellular automata" in the title. The crawls from the 4 brief seeds alone reached 87 (small) and 92 (big). It did this with half as many papers as the big crawl: 2,760 against 5,688. The search and that crawl together took about 20 minutes and used under a tenth of a free OpenAlex key's daily allowance.
  • The crawler has a structural blind spot. OpenAlex has no reference list for 123 of the 210 NCA-titled works (59%). For 2026 papers the share is 51 of 71 (72%). A citation crawl reaches such a paper only if a paper it has already found cites it, or if the paper is a seed. The crawls reached 86% of the NCA works that have references, but at most 17% of those that don't. That is why every crawl missed the 2026 language-model paper, which only ChatGPT found.
  • More papers does not mean more NCA. Doubling the brief-seeded crawl added 3,169 papers but only 5 NCA papers. Broad keywords pulled in regeneration biology and classic cellular automata (CA) from metallurgy. The NCA-only keywords of the search-seeded crawl drifted too: they pulled in about 500 papers on neural-network CA for land-use simulation, which is an older and unrelated field.
  • Data in a crawl still has to be found by someone. A crawl "covers" a domain here if the domain's evidence paper is somewhere in its data. Getting from 2,760 papers to the findings below took analysis on top: counts of the seed citers and their institutions, a network, and topic models. Reading the crawler's own output would not have produced this report.

All three crawls used the same remaining settings: a 0.3 chance of sampling a paper whose title does not match the keywords, sampling weights of 0.3 for year and 1 for centrality, NMF with 12 topics, and the Retraction Watch check enabled. Each crawl ran with 0 API errors. Retraction Watch: the crawler flagged no retracted papers in the small crawl and one in each of the other two. Both are off-topic papers that came in through keyword drift: a 2006 zebrafish caspase-3 paper in the big crawl, and a 2024 land-use simulation paper (CA plus a neural network) in the search-seeded crawl. OpenAlex also marks both as retracted. Cost: nothing was paid. A free OpenAlex key comes with a daily allowance of 10,000 credits, and the shares above are of that allowance. The search-seeded crawl's share is an upper bound, because the daily counter also included the checks that followed it. The whole study used 27% of one day's allowance.

Method notes. The crawler records papers and citation links, but not every field this study needed. Institution names and abstracts for the crawler's papers were therefore looked up in OpenAlex. The "seed citers" are the 239 papers that cite at least one brief seed. All 239 were already in the small crawl, and its data holds 592 citation links among them. OpenAlex under-indexes citations of Distill and arXiv papers, so read counts as relative, not absolute. Its institution matching sometimes drops affiliations; for example, Google is missing from the normalised record of the 2026 reasoning paper. Nothing was checked against Google Scholar.

Topics in the NCA literature

The topic model below uses the best text signal available. The corpus is 361 papers: the 239 seed citers plus the 210 NCA-titled works, with duplicates removed. Of these, 268 are in at least one crawl's data. The other 93 came only from an OpenAlex title search, and the map shows them as hollow dots, so it also shows where the crawls have gaps. Titles and abstracts were embedded with SPECTER, a model trained on scientific papers. The embeddings were reduced with UMAP to 8 dimensions and clustered with k-means. Keywords come from class-based TF-IDF. k=12 was used because its silhouette score (0.468) was almost the same as the best one (k=6, 0.470) and it gives more readable topics. The agent named the topics from their keywords and the papers nearest each cluster centre.

How to read it. The crawls cover games, quality-diversity, graphics and developmental biology almost completely. They cover only about half of NCA dynamics and theory, physical simulation and NCA meets deep learning and language. Those are the newest topics, where papers without reference lists are most common. Clusters 3 and 5 partly act as catch-alls that mix foundational NCA papers with applications. The topic names are the agent's judgement, and a different k moves the boundaries. Treat the model as a map for exploring the field, not as a classification.

What the big crawl added, and why

Same seeds and keywords as the small crawl, with 6 × 50 instead of 3 × 25 papers. It added 3,169 papers but only 5 NCA papers. 83% of the new papers came in as references of a few sampled papers. These were mainly Levin-lab reviews on regeneration and bioelectricity, each with 200–300 references, and classic (non-neural) CA in metallurgy and corrosion. The most common new fields were cell biology (478), artificial intelligence (373), neuroscience (185) and metallurgy (155).

Why: broad keywords such as "regeneration" and "cellular automata" also match work that is not NCA. The big crawl shows which fields NCA draws on. It does not find more NCA work.

What search seeding changed

The search-seeded crawl reached the most NCA-titled works (96) with the fewest papers (2,760). It reached 5 that neither brief-seeded crawl had, but 4 of those were its own seeds. So most of the gain comes from seeding, not from the expansion around the seeds. It lost 1 NCA paper that the brief-seeded crawls had.

It drifted too, in a different way. Of its 1,130 papers that neither brief-seeded crawl had, 526 are about land-use or urban change. Most came in as citers of a single 2002 paper on "neural-network-based cellular automata for simulating land use", which matches the NCA keywords and brought in 862 papers on its own. That paper belongs to an older geography field that shares the name but not the method.

What all three crawls miss

  • Work with no reference list in OpenAlex. This covers most of 2026. Examples are the 2026 language-model paper (W7135075179) and the 2026 Google reasoning paper (W7214964112). Neither can be reached through citations. They get into a crawl only as seeds.
  • Work in other "neural CA" lines that has no citation link to the Mordvintsev lineage. Examples are traffic simulation (W7220892747) and protein folding (W2809252620).
  • Things that are not papers, such as demos, code, startups and student groups.

Only search finds these. Search and the crawler complement each other: search for recency and for seeds, and the crawler to expand them into a full, checkable map.

Answer: is all this machinery better than Claude's search?

For coverage, yes, by a wide margin. For the newest work, no. In about 3 minutes, Claude's web search named 14 good papers and covered 5 of the 14 domains fully. A crawl seeded from those papers took about 17 more minutes and under a tenth of a free OpenAlex key's daily allowance. It turned them into 2,760 papers: 96 NCA-titled works, every seed citer, and evidence for 11 domains fully and 2 partly. The search-only modes missed photonic hardware, gold nanoparticles, edge vision, blood-cell classification and DNA computing, and the crawl reached all five. The crawl also gives every claim an OpenAlex ID, which is how the four ChatGPT errors were caught.

The crawl cannot see papers without reference lists, and those are most of the last year's work. Only search found the 2026 language-model paper. The crawl also drifts into fields that share NCA's keywords. And it does not interpret anything: the findings above came from analysis on top of its data.

Recommended workflow: use agentic search to find seeds and recent work, use the crawler to expand the seeds and check claims, and run an analysis pass on the crawl data. Use focused keywords, and check the largest sources of new papers for drift.

Reproducing this study

The scripts are in docs/studies/2026-10-nca-adopters/scripts/, next to this report in the repository, and data/ holds the numbers behind the figures. The run order is in the folder's README.md. The scripts need OPENALEX_API_KEY in fakenewscitationnetwork/.env. OpenAlex data changes daily, so later runs will give slightly different counts. The 33 papers used as evidence are also in a Zotero collection, exported with the repository's own Zotero export.
